# Why did revenue in Germany drop in Q3 1996 compared with Q3 1995?

Germany's revenue fell 15.1% ($3.73M) from $24.70M to $20.97M in Q3 1996 versus Q3 1995, driven by the loss of three major automobile fleet‑parts customers (Customer#000001502, #000001501, #000001503) that together lost $5.19M (139% of the total change) after losing their annual tenders to a lower‑cost competitor in June 1996 [Q1][Q3][Q6].

## What drove it
The AUTOMOBILE segment dropped $5.56M (149% of the total change) [Q4]. Within it, three accounts went to zero: Customer#000001502 (‑$1.92M, 51%), Customer#000001501 (‑$1.85M, 50%), Customer#000001503 (‑$1.42M, 38%) [Q3][Q7]. These three alone account for $5.19M of lost revenue (139% of the net change).

## Why: what the CRM says
CRM records show all three customers had a “contract_ended” event on 1996‑06‑28, each noting loss of the annual fleet‑parts tender to a lower‑cost competitor, with final deliveries ending June and no orders expected from July [Q6]. Earlier, in March 1996, each had escalated complaints about repeated late deliveries, prompting supplier reviews [Q6].

## Price or volume
The decline was volume‑driven: units sold fell from 23,613 to 20,088 (‑3,525 units) while revenue per unit was nearly flat ($1,045.97 to $1,044.02). Volume effect was ‑$3.69M versus a price effect of ‑$39.2K [Q1].

## Compared with the whole company
Company‑wide revenue declined 4.2% ($13.27M) over the same periods, so Germany’s 15.1% drop exceeded the company‑wide decline by 10.8 percentage points [Q2].

## Ruled out
By order priority, no category moved far enough from the overall trend to be the primary driver; the largest shift was 3‑MEDIUM (+$1.13M, z=3.1), which offset some decline but did not drive the net drop [Q5]. By shipping mode, no category’s change was statistically dominant; the largest was REG AIR (‑$1.06M, z=‑1.3) [Q6]. By part type, no individual category was significant enough to drive the drop; the largest was PROMO POLISHED COPPER (+$221.1K, z=2.5) [Q9].

## Confidence and limitations
95% confidence. A concentrated driver was found (three customers = 139% of change); CRM events corroborate the mechanism (tender losses); the main driver was drilled into (automobile segment and specific accounts); compared with the company‑wide trend; all critic checks passed. Open points: none.
